The nine boxes of the business, **each one a hypothesis with a state**. This is not a business plan: it is the map of what is known, what is believed, and what nobody has tested. It changes when evidence arrives.

The **why** behind each product choice lives in `DECISIONS.md` and is not repeated here (D12) — this document is the **business** lens: who pays, how much, why, and what is still unknown.

---

## The three states

| State | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Validated** | Put to the test against reality and passed. The evidence is named. |
| **Untested** | A reasonable belief that nothing has confronted. The test is named and has not run. |
| **Invalidated** | Tested and failed. It stays written — it is what stops the project rediscovering it. |

Three states, no more. A box nobody has answered yet, a number nobody has measured, and a choice nobody has made are all **untested** — what separates them is the *How it's tested* cell, not a fourth label. Resist inventing states like "half validated": if half a box is proven and half is not, the box holds two hypotheses and should be written as two.

An **invalidated** box is never deleted. It is the most expensive knowledge in the document.

## Box sections

Every box carries the same five things. Keep them short — a box that needs pages is hiding two hypotheses.

- **State** — one of the three, on its own line.
- **The hypothesis** — what is believed, in plain words.
- **What supports it** — the evidence, named. "It is obvious" is not evidence.
- **What does not** — the honest limit of that evidence: sample of one, reasoning rather than measurement, a number nobody has.
- **How it's tested** — the specific thing that would settle it, and who owns the answer. Cheap tests are named as cheap.
